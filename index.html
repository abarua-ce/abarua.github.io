About

I build numerical models of how CO2 moves, dissolves, and is trapped deep underground — physics that matters for safe, long-term carbon storage. My work sits at the intersection of subsurface flow physics, finite-element methods, and high-performance scientific computing.

Most of my day-to-day is spent inside Proteus, an open-source toolkit for computational methods, where I develop and verify a fully-coupled compositional two-phase (CO2–brine) flow solver against community benchmarks such as the FluidFlower international comparison study.

Research

My research develops and verifies numerical methods for multiphase, multicomponent flow in geologic formations, with a focus on the accuracy and robustness needed for field-scale CO2 storage.

Compositional CO2–brine flow

A fully-coupled two-component formulation in pressure–overall-composition (p, z) variables with an analytic phase-equilibrium (flash) closure, capturing dissolution and exsolution without ad-hoc kinetics.

Stabilized finite elements

Entropy-viscosity and flux-corrected transport schemes that keep sharp saturation fronts monotone and mass-conservative across strong capillary and permeability discontinuities.

Benchmark verification

Method-of-manufactured-solution and analytical checks (McWhorter–Sunada), plus validation against the FluidFlower laboratory-scale international benchmark.
